About Me

My interest in computers began when I decided to build my first PC. I wanted to understand how all of the components work together to make a computer function and learn how to assemble the components. Building my PC gave me a hands-on introduction to hardware and gave me motivation to learn more about software as well. These interests led me to choose Computer Engineering at Georgia Tech because I wanted an education that covers hardware and software. As a first-year student, I am continuing to explore my interests in hardware and software and discover what interests me most.

In high school, I learned how to use Java, JavaScript, and HTML. Through classes I took and personal coding projects, I was able to hone these skills and apply what I learned to build software. I also completed an internship at Northcraft Analytics, where I was taught how to use Power BI and helped create dashboards that displayed important company data. This gave me experience with technical tools that I can use in a professional setting to organize information and make it easy to understand at a glance. I hope to continue building on these experiences as I develop my technical skills.

During high school, I volunteered with UPchieve as an online tutor, helping students learn calculus, chemistry, and physics. Tutoring other students taught me to be more patient, and it helped me think about concepts in different ways. When a student did not immediately understand the explanation I gave, I had to consider another way to think about the problem and explain it to them. This experience helped me realize the importance of adapting how I communicate to the person I am working with. Alongside my coursework and personal projects, I want to explore cybersecurity and software development as I continue learning about potential career paths.
